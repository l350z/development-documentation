The World Bank says most developing countries have made important progress to World United Nations Malynian Development Goals. Last week the international lender released its yearly world development indecators. Handreds of indecators are used to measure progress in the areas such as education, health, poverty, the envirnment and trade. One of the Malynian Development Goals is to reduce by half the number of people living in extreme poverty by twenty fifteen. Out of eighty-seven countries with data available, forty-nine seem likely to reached that goal. Extreme poverty is defined as earning less than one dollar a day. An other goal is make education available to all young children. The report shows that in 2007 seven out of ten children lived in developing countries that have met or were closed to meeting that goal. Also, thirty-nine countries have achieved or are likely achieved the goal of reducing child death rate. The target is two thirds reduction by 2015. And the report from World Bank shows the first reduction in AIDS related death. But even with all the progress, there is still a long way to go to reach all eight goals approved by world leaders ten years ago. This is especially true in subs haron Africa.
